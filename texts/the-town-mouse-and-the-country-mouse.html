The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse

A country mouse lived in a quiet field at the edge of a small village. One autumn day she invited her cousin, a town mouse, to visit her. The town mouse came, and the country mouse was glad to see her.

She set out everything she had: a few dry peas, some barley, a piece of old cheese and a handful of nuts. The town mouse nibbled at the food politely, but she wrinkled her nose.

"My poor cousin," she said at last, "how can you live like this? Don't you get tired of plain food and hard work? Come with me to London, and I'll show you how to live well."

The country mouse agreed, and that evening they set off together. Late at night they reached a large house. In the dining room the table was still covered with the half-eaten remains of a great feast: soft white bread, sweet cakes, jelly, honey and fine cheese.

The country mouse had never seen such food. She began to eat, and she thought that her cousin was right. But suddenly the door opened, and two servants came in with a lamp. The mice ran and hid in a dark hole in the wall. They waited, trembling, until the room was quiet again.

When they crept out, a huge cat jumped onto the table. The cat's green eyes shone in the dark. Again they had to run for their lives.

The country mouse took her coat and her little bag. "Goodbye, cousin," she said. "You may keep your cakes and your honey. I'd rather eat dry peas in peace than eat a feast in fear."
